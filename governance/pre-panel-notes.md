# Pre-panel notes - what the author already believes is wrong

*Written 2026-10-12, BEFORE the Rule 7.4 panel was spawned. This exists so N,
the novel share of panel defects, can be measured rather than reconstructed.*

**Authorship, stated plainly.** The two exhibits were built by the Phase 1
Claude Code session from the accepted v5 package, restyled to the Cascadia
standard; Aaron Robbins is the owner and has not yet seen a render. The
session read its own renders at 1440 and 320 px before writing this list and
fixed what it found before any seat existed (recorded below as withdrawn).
Anything the panel also finds that is listed here is **not** novel.

## Suspected, exhibit by exhibit

**The V (initial state, decision-rule pair selected)**

1. The dashed pairing lines are the diagram's whole point and the weakest
   mark on it: 1 px Rain for the unselected pairs sits at 2.45:1 on Paper and
   may read as a gridline or not at all, so a reader may see three columns of
   boxes rather than three pairs.
2. The numbers 1 to 7 run down the left and up the right, but nothing says
   the sequence is a path; a reader may take "7 / Intended use" as a seventh,
   separate thing rather than the check that closes the first promise.
3. "AI agents work to the specification" under "Build the queue" is the only
   mention of AI on the canvas, and a reader may take the diagram to be about
   agents rather than about pairing promises with checks.
4. The detail panel below the V repeats the selected pair; a reader may not
   realise it changes when a node is clicked, and the static render cannot
   show that it does (Rule 7.4 scope clause: this is interaction, outside the
   panel).
5. At 320 px the middle caption ("Each check traces back to its promise") is
   hidden and the only statement of the pairing idea is the figcaption below;
   the seats were told which render is the phone one and may notice the loss.
6. The title "Specify the promise. Plan its proof." is an instruction, not a
   finding; 7.1's test ("is the sentence the title?") may fail for the
   legitimate reason that the exhibit asserts a method, not a number.

**The Experiment 1 queue (all three controls removed)**

7. "3 entries" against "2 orders" is the comparison the exhibit wants made,
   and the two boxes sit above the bars in Felix styling, not inside the
   Cascadia frame; a seat reading the frame alone may quote a count from the
   row decisions instead and may miss the specified answer entirely.
8. Three rows are Madrona for three different reasons (boundary missed,
   canceled but included, repeated copy); the status text carries the
   difference but the colour does not, and a reader may report "three
   failures of the same kind."
9. The 48-hour tick at 60% of the track is unlabeled on the bar; its meaning
   is in the key line under the chart, and a reader who skips the key may ask
   what the vertical line is.
10. The age column is a fixed 0 to 80 scale with no axis; a reader may
    wonder why 80, or read the bars as proportions of something.
11. "A105 / Copy of row 5" is the duplicate; a reader may take the two A105
    rows as two different orders that happen to share an ID, which is the
    very confusion the exhibit is about, and may count five included rather
    than three entries.
12. The provenance strip's fourth segment names three removed controls in
    prose; at 320 px it runs to four lines and may be skipped.
